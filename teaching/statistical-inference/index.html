Overview

Statistics is the science of learning from data in the presence of uncertainty. The course gives the foundations of classical statistical inference (how to estimate unknown quantities, how precise those estimates are, and how to test hypotheses about them) and then moves to prediction, in the spirit of An Introduction to Statistical Learning: multiple regression, flexible non-linear models, resampling methods, uncertainty quantification for predictions (including conformal prediction), and classification. Every topic is developed on the board, illustrated with real data, and implemented in R during the weekly lab.

Prerequisites

  • Mathematics: logarithms and powers and their properties; derivatives and integrals; basic linear algebra (vectors, matrices, matrix products).
  • Probability and statistics: mean, variance, covariance and correlation and their properties; random variables; PDF, CDF, quantiles and conditional densities; the main probability distributions; simple regression.
  • Software: basic experience with R (it will be revised in the first labs). Please install R and RStudio on your laptop and bring it to the lab sessions.

Learning outcomes

  1. Knowledge and understanding. Students will acquire a solid understanding of the principles of statistical inference (point and interval estimation, hypothesis testing and statistical modelling) and of the basic tools of multivariate analysis and statistical learning.
  2. Applying knowledge and understanding. Students will be able to analyse real data in R, choosing appropriate methods for estimation, testing, regression, prediction and classification, and to translate practical problems into suitable statistical models.
  3. Making judgements. Students will be able to assess critically the assumptions behind a model, recognise its limitations and potential biases, and judge the validity of the conclusions, including the trade-off between model complexity and predictive accuracy.
  4. Communication skills. Students will be able to present the results of a statistical analysis clearly and rigorously, using correct statistical and probabilistic language.
  5. Learning skills. Students will be able to study more advanced methods of statistical inference and statistical learning on their own.

Programme

The course has seven parts over twelve weeks. Weeks are indicative and will be updated as the course progresses.

Week Part Topics
1 0. Aspects of statistics Probability vs statistical inference; why we need to quantify uncertainty. Random samples, sampling variability, sample statistics and the sampling distribution of the sample mean. The statistical model and an overview of the main inferential problems. R refresher.
2–3 1. Point estimation Properties of estimators: unbiasedness, mean squared error, efficiency, asymptotic properties (consistency). How to construct estimators: the likelihood function, maximum likelihood estimation (Bernoulli example in R), the relative likelihood; method of moments.
4–5 2. Interval estimation Accuracy, curvature and Fisher information; the Cramér–Rao bound; properties of maximum likelihood estimators. Likelihood sets. Confidence intervals: interpretation, intervals from the sample mean and from the MLE.
6–7 3. Hypothesis testing The logic and the steps of a test; type I and type II errors; p-values. Optimality: the power function, Neyman–Pearson lemma, uniformly most powerful tests, likelihood ratio tests; sample size determination. Comparing two means.
8–9 4. Linear regression Simple linear regression: OLS estimation and its properties, tests and confidence intervals for the coefficients, goodness of fit (R²), confidence vs prediction intervals. Multiple linear regression: the model in matrix form, OLS estimation and the Gauss–Markov theorem, interpretation of the coefficients, t and F tests, qualitative predictors and interactions, model checking and residual diagnostics.
10 5. Beyond linear regression Moving beyond linearity: polynomial regression, step functions, regression splines and smoothing splines, local regression, generalised additive models.
11 6. Resampling, prediction and uncertainty quantification Training vs test error and the bias–variance trade-off. Resampling methods: cross-validation and the bootstrap. Uncertainty in prediction: prediction intervals beyond the linear model, from bootstrap-based intervals to conformal prediction.
12 7. Classification The classification problem; logistic regression; k-nearest neighbours; decision trees and random forests; confusion matrix and ROC curve.

    Books and study resources

    Slides, lab notebooks (R) and exercise sheets will be published weekly, before each lecture, on the course e-learning page.

    • Cicchitelli, D’Urso, Minozzo, Statistics: Principles and Methods, Pearson, 2021. Main textbook: Chapters 17–23.
    • R Web Appendix: Agresti, Kateri, Foundations of Statistics for Data Scientists.
    • James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani, An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Applications in R, 2nd ed., Springer, 2021 (free PDF on the book website).

    R, RStudio and R Markdown

    • N. Matloff, The Art of R Programming, No Starch Press, 2011.
    • R Markdown: the definitive guide (free online), for writing reports and homework that combine text, code and results.

    Assessment

    The assessment scheme of AY 2025/2026 is reported below; details for AY 2026/2027 will be confirmed before the start of the course.

    • Final written exam (70%) on the first part of the course: theoretical questions, short proofs seen in class, and exercises (computations and interpretation of R output).
    • Homework with R Markdown (30%) on the second part of the course, in groups of 1–3 students. Collaboration is encouraged!
    • Oral exam if the written exam or the homework is not sufficient or not done.
    • Bonus: +1 point on the final grade for taking part in the Q&A section of the Moodle page.

    Examples of exam questions are shared in class, and the last week includes a mock examination. Exam dates are published on Infostud and on my Sapienza lecturer page.
